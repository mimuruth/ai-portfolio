## Engineering Lab 12 — Rehearsing the defence

### The mental model

An interview answer is a **proof sketch**: claim (the number), method (the design
decision), and honesty about scope (the trade-off). Vague enthusiasm reads as
uncertainty; a specific number followed by the reasoning behind it reads as
ownership.

> **Production note — the honesty that reads as senior.** "If DPO doesn't beat SFT,
> that's a real finding — the preference pairs weren't discriminative, and the fix
> is better data, not more epochs." Admitting a limitation with the correct next
> step signals more experience than claiming everything worked.

### Hands-on lab

1. Record yourself giving the 30-second pitch for each of the four repos from
   §12.1–12.4. Time it; cut until it fits.
2. Take five questions from the §12.5 bank and answer each in the three-beat
   cadence out loud, without notes.
3. Prepare one "a failure I diagnosed and fixed" story per project, ending with the
   metric that confirmed the fix (e.g., the `nan`-as-pass eval-gate bug).

### Review & exercises

- **Scenario.** "Why not just fine-tune the model on the company's documents?" Give
  the one-sentence answer and the single deciding principle behind it.
- **Scenario.** "How would you cut this system's cost in half?" Answer using your
  measured unit cost and name the first lever you would pull.
- **System design.** Sketch, in five bullets, how you would scale `prod-rag` to 100×
  traffic, and which metric you would alert on first.
